Do steroids cause weight gain?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Yes, steroids can cause weight gain. Corticosteroids like prednisone often lead to increased appetite, fluid retention, and fat redistribution—typically around the face, abdomen, and back of the neck. Anabolic steroids can also increase weight, primarily through muscle mass gain and water retention.

The amount of weight gained depends on the type of steroid, dosage, and duration of use. Long-term or high-dose corticosteroid use is more likely to cause noticeable weight changes, while short courses may have minimal impact.

Weight gain isn't the only possible side effect, and other symptoms may signal that your body is reacting in ways worth investigating. Because steroid-related changes can overlap with other health conditions, it's important to understand exactly what's driving your symptoms before deciding on next steps.
