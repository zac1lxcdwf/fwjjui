What does it mean if my face is numb?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Facial numbness is often caused by nerve-related conditions like trigeminal neuropathy or by reduced blood flow in the carotid artery. Other potential causes include Bell's palsy, migraines, multiple sclerosis, shingles, or, in more urgent cases, a stroke.

Explanation

Feeling numbness in your face might be linked to problems with the nerves that help you feel sensations, like the trigeminal nerve. This is called trigeminal neuropathy, and it can make parts of your face feel numb or tingly. Sometimes, facial numbness can also be a sign that there is a problem with blood flow in the carotid artery, which is a blood vessel in your neck that supplies blood to your face and brain.

Possible Causes of Facial Numbness:

[Numb What](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/)

Trigeminal Neuropathy: This condition affects the trigeminal nerve, which is responsible for feeling in your face. When this nerve is not working well, you might feel numbness or tingling.

Carotid Artery Issues: Problems with the carotid artery can reduce blood flow to your face, leading to numbness or other unusual feelings.
