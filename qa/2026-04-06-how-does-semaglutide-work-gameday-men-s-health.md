What Is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide belongs to a class of medications known as GLP-1 receptor agonists. These medications mimic a hormone that the body naturally produces after eating. By activating the same biological pathways, semaglutide helps regulate appetite, digestion, and blood sugar levels. (1)

Most commonly, semaglutide is administered as a once-weekly injection. The injection allows the medication to enter the bloodstream gradually and maintain steady activity throughout the week. This long-acting design is one reason semaglutide has become increasingly widely used in medical weight loss programs in recent years.

Originally developed to treat type 2 diabetes, semaglutide was first used to help patients improve blood sugar control. During clinical trials, researchers noticed that many participants also experienced significant weight loss. What Is GLP-1 and Why Does It Matter?

GLP-1 stands for glucagon-like peptide-1, a hormone that the body naturally releases after eating food.

GLP-1 plays several important roles in regulating metabolism and appetite. It acts as a messenger between the digestive system, pancreas, and brain. These signals help the body manage energy intake and blood sugar levels more effectively. (3)

One of GLP-1’s key functions is appetite signaling. When food enters the digestive system, GLP-1 sends signals to the brain that contribute to feelings of fullness. This helps prevent overeating and allows the body to regulate calorie intake.

The hormone also influences blood sugar control. It helps stimulate insulin release when glucose levels rise, while also slowing the release of glucagon, another hormone that increases blood sugar.

GLP-1 contributes to several metabolic processes, including: (4)

Signaling fullness to the brain after meals

Supporting insulin release when blood sugar rises

Slowing the movement of food through the stomach

Helping maintain balanced blood glucose levels

Semaglutide works by mimicking this natural hormone and amplifying its effects.

How Semaglutide Affects Appetite and Satiety

One of the most noticeable effects of semaglutide is reduced appetite. Many patients report that they feel full sooner during meals and think about food less frequently throughout the day.

This change is largely driven by the medication’s interaction with appetite centers in the brain. By activating GLP-1 receptors in these areas, semaglutide helps regulate hunger signals that influence eating behavior.

Another important factor is slower gastric emptying. Gastric emptying refers to the process by which food moves from the stomach into the small intestine. Semaglutide can slow down this process slightly, which allows food to remain in the stomach longer.

How Semaglutide Impacts Blood Sugar and Metabolism

Beyond appetite control, semaglutide also plays an important role in blood sugar regulation, which is one of the main reasons it was originally developed for the management of diabetes.

When blood glucose levels rise after a meal, semaglutide helps stimulate the pancreas to release insulin. (5) Insulin allows cells to absorb glucose from the bloodstream and use it for energy. This process prevents blood sugar from remaining elevated for long periods.

At the same time, semaglutide reduces the release of glucagon. Glucagon is a hormone that signals the liver to release stored glucose into the bloodstream. By limiting glucagon when blood sugar is already high, semaglutide helps prevent unnecessary spikes. (6)

This balanced hormonal response contributes to more stable blood glucose levels throughout the day. Stable blood sugar can help reduce energy crashes and cravings that sometimes occur when glucose levels fluctuate rapidly.

Improved blood sugar regulation can also support overall metabolic health. Many patients notice improvements in markers such as fasting glucose and insulin sensitivity during supervised treatment.

Does Semaglutide Burn Fat?

A common misconception about semaglutide is that it directly burns fat in the body. While weight loss often occurs during treatment, the mechanism behind that loss is indirect.

Semaglutide primarily works by reducing appetite and calorie intake. When people eat fewer calories than their bodies require for daily energy needs, the body begins using stored energy sources. These sources include body fat.

In other words, while the medication does not function as a traditional “fat burner,” semaglutide helps create the conditions that make fat loss possible.

Several factors influence how much fat loss occurs during treatment. These include diet quality, activity levels, and how consistent each patient is when it comes to taking the medication.

Maintaining a calorie deficit over time
