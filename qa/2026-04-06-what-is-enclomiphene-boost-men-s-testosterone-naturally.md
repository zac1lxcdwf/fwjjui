As a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), enclomiphene exhibits tissue-specific effects, acting as an antagonist (blocking the effects) of estrogen in certain tissues while mimicking the effects of estrogen in others.

In the context of men’s health, enclomiphene selectively blocks estrogen receptors in the hypothalamus and pituitary gland. By doing so, it prevents the negative feedback loop that estrogen exerts on the production of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), LH, and FSH. This disruption leads to an increase in the secretion of these hormones, which stimulate the testes to produce more testosterone.

The increased LH production directly stimulates the Leydig cells in the testes, which are responsible for testosterone synthesis. Meanwhile, the elevated FSH levels support spermatogenesis, the process of sperm production. This dual action of enclomiphene on both testosterone production and sperm generation makes it a promising treatment option for men with hypogonadism or fertility issues.
