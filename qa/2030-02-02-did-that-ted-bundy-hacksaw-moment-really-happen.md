There are a number of disturbing moments in the Ted Bundy movie Extremely Wicked, Shockingly Evil and Vile, but there's one scene in particular—right at the very end—that has fans buzzing. You know the one we mean. The "hacksaw" scene.

Right before the Netflix film wraps up, Bundy's ex-girlfriend Elizabeth Kloepfer comes to visit him in prison. Desperate for Bundy to take responsibility for the terrible crimes he's been convicted of, Kloepfer begs him to tell her the truth—specifically, how and why one of his victims was found beheaded. Bundy won't say anything into the phone; instead, he fogs up the glass and writes "hacksaw" with his finger—the first time in the film he ever admits to any kind of guilt.

Did Ted Bundy really write hacksaw?

No, Bundy never wrote hacksaw on the prison glass. In fact, it's unknown if Kloepfer ever even visited Bundy in jail. The film's director, Joe Berlinger, told Decider that he took some "dramatic license" during the scene:

"The final moment between Zac [Efron] and Lily [Collins] was actually a phone call in the memoir. It was not an in-person visit on death row. So that was the most extreme example of taking dramatic license. Because of the era that we’re in of holding perpetrators accountable—which is something I obviously believe in—it was very important for me to make much more of that moment than is in [Kloepfer’s] memoir. So, her visiting him on death row, holding him accountable, making him admit to the degree that she makes him admit—that’s dramatic license. In the memoir, that conversation is more obtuse. He never really admits to her to the degree that we see it in the movie. She was satisfied that it was an admission, but it wasn’t nearly as dramatic or specific as we show in the movie."

While the hacksaw moment wasn't real, it is based on one of Bundy's real murders.
