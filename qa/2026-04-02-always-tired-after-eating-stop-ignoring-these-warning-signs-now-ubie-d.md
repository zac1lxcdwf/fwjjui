Why do I feel always tired after eating a meal?

Feeling tired after eating, often called postprandial somnolence or a "food coma," is common and usually harmless. The main causes include:

High-sugar or high-fat meals, which trigger insulin spikes and blood sugar crashes

Large portion sizes, which redirect blood flow to digestion

Foods rich in tryptophan or carbohydrates, which boost serotonin and melatonin

Occasional post-meal fatigue is normal. However, if it happens consistently, feels severe, or is paired with symptoms like bloating, brain fog, dizziness, or weight changes, it may point to something worth investigating.

Explanation

After eating, feeling tired can be linked to the kinds of foods consumed. Foods high in fats and carbohydrates might make you feel sleepy. One study showed that these nutrients can influence fatigue after meals. Sugary snacks can also lead to a quick burst of energy followed by fatigue.

To manage post-meal tiredness, consider these tips:

Eat Balanced Meals: Include a mix of proteins, fats, and carbohydrates.

Watch Portion Sizes: Large meals can make you feel more tired.

Stay Hydrated: Drink water throughout the day.
