About the Symptom

Changes in stool smell can be caused by the foods you eat, intolerances or allergies, infections and underlying medical conditions. Abnormally foul-smelling stools may also be a sign of an underlying disease, such as celiac disease, Crohn's disease, cystic fibrosis, and intestinal infections.

Possible Causes

Generally, Change in stool smell can be related to:

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is a genetic disorder that causes severe damage to the lungs, digestive system, and other organs in the body.

A condition where the intestines can't absorb or reabsorb protein well, leading to low blood protein levels. This causes symptoms like water retention in the legs, lungs, and abdomen.

Related Serious Diseases

Sometimes, Change in stool smell may be related to these serious diseases:

An infection of the large intestine, typically following antibiotic use. The antibiotics kill both good and bad bacteria, disrupting the normal balance of gut flora.

Doctor's Diagnostic Questions

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this symptom:

Does your stool have a different or strange smell?

Do your stools have an offensive smell?

Do your stools smell sour?
