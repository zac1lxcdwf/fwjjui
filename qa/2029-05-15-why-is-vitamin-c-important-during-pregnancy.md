# Why Is Vitamin C Important During Pregnancy?

Why Is Vitamin C Important During Pregnancy?

Pregnancy is an exciting and often overwhelming time for expectant mothers. With so many changes happening in the body, it is important to ensure you are taking the best care of yourself and your growing baby. One key part is ensuring you get enough vitamin C during pregnancy.

We will discuss why vitamin C is important for pregnant women, how to increase your intake, and what potential risks to be aware of.

Why is Vitamin C important for pregnant women?

Vitamin C is an essential vitamin that is necessary for many bodily functions and plays a role in keeping us healthy. It helps keep our bones, teeth, and gums strong and healthy, and it is important for iron absorption from our diet. Vitamin C is also an antioxidant, which helps protect our cells from damage caused by free radicals.

For pregnant women, Vitamin C plays an even more important role. It helps to support the body during pregnancy by assisting with fetal growth, increasing resistance to infection, and supporting collagen formation, which is needed for the baby’s cartilage, bone, and skin. Vitamin C also plays a role in helping the mother’s body produce progesterone, which is needed for a healthy pregnancy.

Vitamin C plays a role in protecting the mother and baby from infection and other health risks. It also helps the body to properly produce the hormones needed for a healthy pregnancy.

How much Vitamin C do pregnant women need?

Women need more Vitamin C during pregnancy as the body’s demands for this vitamin increase. The recommended daily intake of Vitamin C during pregnancy is 85 mg. This is slightly higher than the 75 mg daily recommended intake for non-pregnant individuals.

Consuming too much Vitamin C during pregnancy may result in stomach upset, gas, and diarrhea. Additionally, excess intake of Vitamin C can lead to an increase in iron absorption, which may be harmful to both mother and baby if not monitored properly. Therefore, staying within the recommended daily intake is best to avoid any potential risks.

Food sources of Vitamin C for pregnant women

To ensure you get enough Vitamin C, it is important to incorporate foods rich in this vitamin into your diet.

Fruits are among the best sources of Vitamin C and include citrus fruits such as oranges, lemons, limes, grapefruits, and tangerines.

Other good sources of Vitamin C include berries such as strawberries, raspberries, blueberries, and blackberries. Cantaloupe, kiwi, and papaya are also excellent sources of Vitamin C.

Vegetables are also a great source of Vitamin C, with some of the best sources being bell peppers, spinach, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, kale, collard greens, and tomatoes.

Some legumes and grains are also good sources of Vitamin C, including beans, peas, lentils, oats, barley, and quinoa.

Maintaining a well-balanced diet during pregnancy is important to ensure that you are getting enough Vitamin C. Eating a variety of these foods will ensure you get all the essential nutrients you need for your baby’s development and health. Additionally, talking to your doctor or healthcare provider about any supplements you may want to take while pregnant is important.

Supplementation of Vitamin C during pregnancy

Pregnant women may benefit from taking Vitamin C supplements to ensure they are getting enough of this vitamin in their diets. Supplementation can be beneficial for pregnant women who do not consume enough fruits and vegetables to get adequate amounts of Vitamin C, as well as for women with preexisting medical conditions such as anemia or gastrointestinal issues that can make it difficult to absorb Vitamin C from food sources.

When taking Vitamin C supplements during pregnancy, adhering to the recommended dosage is important. Too much Vitamin C can lead to side effects such as diarrhea, abdominal pain, and nausea, so pregnant women should consult with their healthcare providers before supplementing.
