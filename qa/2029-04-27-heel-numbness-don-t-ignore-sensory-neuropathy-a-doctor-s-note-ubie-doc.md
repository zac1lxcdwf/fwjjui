Understanding Sensory Neuropathy in the Heel of the Foot

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sensory neuropathy in the heel typically causes buzzing, tingling, numbness, or burning pain that may worsen over time and affect balance. Common causes include diabetes, repetitive stress, vitamin deficiencies, and nerve compression.

Diagnosis and treatment depend on several factors, including blood tests, nerve conduction studies, lifestyle adjustments, and supportive therapies. Because heel neuropathy can stem from many underlying conditions—some serious—identifying the root cause early is critical to preventing long-term nerve damage.

Explanation

If you've ever experienced a constant "buzzing" in heels, you're not alone. Sensory neuropathy affecting the heel can lead to a range of uncomfortable feelings—from tingling and numbness to burning and sharp pain. By understanding the causes, symptoms, and management options, you can take steps toward relief and better foot health.

What Is Sensory Neuropathy?

Sensory neuropathy refers to damage or dysfunction of the sensory nerves that relay information—such as touch, temperature, and pain—from your feet to your brain. When the nerves in or around your heel are affected, you may notice:

A constant "buzzing" in heels

Tingling or "pins and needles"

Numbness or reduced sensation

Burning or sharp, stabbing pains

Sensitivity to light touch or temperature changes

Neuropathy can be mild or progress to more severe symptoms. Early recognition and intervention can help prevent complications.

Common Causes of Heel Neuropathy

Diabetes Mellitus• Chronically high blood sugar can damage nerve fibers over time, leading to diabetic neuropathy.• Poor circulation compounds the problem, slowing nerve repair.

Mechanical Pressure and Repetitive Stress• Wearing ill-fitting shoes or spending long hours standing can compress the nerves around your heel.• Activities like running, jumping or walking on hard surfaces may irritate the plantar nerves.

Tarsal Tunnel Syndrome• Similar to carpal tunnel in the wrist, this occurs when the tibial nerve is compressed as it passes through a narrow tunnel on the inside of the ankle.• May cause shooting pains, tingling or that constant "buzzing" in heels.

Vitamin Deficiencies• Lack of vitamins B1 (thiamine), B6, B12, or vitamin E can interfere with nerve health and repair.

Chronic Kidney or Liver Disease• Toxins that aren't cleared effectively can accumulate and damage peripheral nerves.

Autoimmune and Inflammatory Conditions• Disorders such as rheumatoid arthritis or lupus may involve nerve inflammation.

Medications and Toxins• Certain chemotherapy drugs, alcohol abuse or exposure to heavy metals can injure nerve cells.

Recognizing the Symptoms

Sensory neuropathy in the heel often starts subtly and may worsen over time. Watch for:

A persistent buzzing or humming sensation in both heels or one side

Burning, aching, or electric-shock–like pains, especially at night

Heightened sensitivity—light touch or bed sheets brushing your feet feels painful

Numb areas where you can't feel hot, cold or sharp objects

Difficulty walking due to loss of balance or coordination

Detailed medical and family history

Treatment Options

While complete reversal of nerve damage isn't always possible, many strategies can reduce symptoms and improve quality of life:

Address Underlying Conditions

Optimize blood sugar control in diabetes through diet, exercise and medications

Treat vitamin deficiencies with supplements under medical guidance

Manage autoimmune diseases with appropriate therapies

Medications for Symptom Relief

Anticonvulsants (e.g., gabapentin, pregabalin) to calm nerve firing

SNRIs or tricyclic antidepressants for pain modulation

Topical agents (capsaicin cream, lidocaine patches) for localized relief

Balance training to reduce the risk of falls

Footwear and Orthotics

Supportive shoes with good cushioning to reduce pressure on the heel

Custom orthotic inserts to improve foot alignment and distribute weight evenly

Lifestyle Modifications

Maintain a healthy weight to ease stress on your feet

Limit alcohol intake and avoid smoking to support nerve health

Pace activities and take breaks to prevent overuse

Self-Care Tips at Home

Inspect your feet daily for cuts, blisters or calluses—neuropathy can reduce your ability to sense injury.

Apply moisturizer to prevent dry, cracked skin but avoid areas between toes where moisture can foster infection.

Soak feet in warm (not hot) water to soothe discomfort; check water temperature with your hands first.

Practice relaxation techniques—yoga, meditation or gentle massage—to help manage chronic pain.

Preventing Further Nerve Damage

Control chronic conditions: Keep blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol in target ranges.

Eat a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins and whole grains.

Stay active with low-impact exercises like swimming or cycling.

Get regular checkups with your primary doctor, podiatrist and eye specialist if you have diabetes.

When to See a Doctor

Seek prompt medical attention if you experience:

Sudden loss of sensation or weakness in your foot

Open sores or ulcers that won't heal

Signs of infection (redness, swelling, warmth, drainage)

Severe, unrelenting heel pain that limits your mobility

Any symptoms that could be life-threatening or require urgent care

Always speak to a doctor about anything that feels serious. Early intervention can prevent complications and protect your long-term foot health.

Living with sensory neuropathy in the heel can be challenging, but by understanding the condition, addressing underlying causes, and adopting appropriate treatments, you can manage symptoms effectively. If you notice a constant "buzzing" in heels or related sensations, take proactive steps—reach out to your healthcare provider and explore supportive therapies to keep you on your feet and enjoying daily activities.
