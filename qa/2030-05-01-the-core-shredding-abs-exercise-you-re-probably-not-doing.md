The key move that gymnasts do to forge a rock-solid core isn’t the situp, crunch, or even the plank. In fact, it’s a move you might not have ever seen in the gym.

[You re Probably Not](https://regeneration-desk.hbsnzj.com/)

The hollow hold is one of the first movements that gymnasts learn. It’s a simple yet tremendously effective exercise for fortifying the core and laying a robust foundation for the rigors—and inherent dangers—of a dynamic training regimen.

Yet while the hollow hold is relatively simple in theory, putting it into practice, especially initially, can be tricky or even downright frustrating—getting, and keeping, your lower back flush against the floor doesn’t come naturally to everyone at first.

That’s where Bree Branker, a trainer at Akin’s Army in New York City, comes in. The core is like the foundation of a house, Branker says in the video above before hitting the deck in the MH Rec Room to demonstrate the hollow hold, one of her favorite moves. “If you have a strong foundation, your house is going to be sturdy.”

From starting out with your knees lifted to maintaining elevated shoulders, Branker coaches you through each step of the hollow hold setup to ensure you know exactly what this move should look and feel like.

And for those who have already mastered this movement and are looking for a challenge, she’s got some variations for you to turn your hollow hold into a next-level core workout by engaging more of your obliques.
