# Are You Making These Holiday Training Mistakes? Here’s How to Fix Them to Maintain Your Gains.

AH, THE HOLIDAYS—a time of wintry cheer and culinary indulgence that’s full of homebound travel and the festive company of family and friends. And with so much celebrating to do and acquaintances to see, it’s also the perfect time to ease off your training and deload for a bit, right?

“Wrong,” says MH fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. “You’re likely going to spend the next couple of months in a slight calorie surplus, but instead of backing off in your training, take advantage of the situation by continuing to train hard.”

Not only will that help you avoid padding your belly with extra insulation, but it will also help you use those extra calories to fuel your muscle-building machinery. To be clear, we aren’t suggesting that you shouldn’t adjust your workouts to better suit your travel and party schedules—just that when you do, you do it strategically and with your objectives and goals clearly in mind.

In particular, you should avoid these four mistakes—then follow the plan laid out in the MH Holiday Strong program, which gives you the diet and training plans you need to succeed this holiday season.

Don't Stay Married to Your Split Routine

Focusing on split training (e.g., body parts, push/pull/legs, upper/lower) is an excellent way to maximize your muscles’ growth stimulus and fast-track your progress toward your strength goals. But in most cases, it also requires you to hit the gym four to six days a week, which might be unrealistic during the holidays.

That’s especially true if you’ll be traveling a lot and having to navigate the equipment in unfamiliar gyms and the unpredictable whims of well-intentioned loved ones. “When that happens, I like to get away from the classic split system and drop it down to two to three full-body workouts a week,” says Samuel.

Depend on Full-Body Workouts

In so doing, you can continue training hard while adopting a workout plan that better suits your holiday schedule. Focusing on full-body workouts also ensures that you’ll hit all of your muscle groups multiple times a week, avoiding the worry and regret that comes with missing a split workout.

Don't Overbook Yourself

“Overbooking” yourself is one of the greatest mental mistakes you can make during the holidays, says Samuel, who admits that he’s often guilty of this common misstep. “There’s this mentality where you’re taking time off from work and there’s less stress, so you’re going to train even harder and work out five or even six days a week,” he explains.

What you might not be considering, however, is that gyms are closed more often during this time of year. Plus, you might not have access to the best equipment while traveling. “As a result, you’re not always going to get in the quality of workout you want,” says Samuel.

Stay Flexible (With Smart Full-Body Workouts)

Expand the scope of your workouts to more comprehensive sessions that challenge your whole body.

If you stick to a full-body plan and discover some extra time here and there to get your sweat on, you can fill that time by doing cardio or mobility training. Think of it as “bonus” work—having already carved out time for your primary workouts, the additional sessions represent a body-boosting gift that will help you reach your goals even faster.

Don't Train Super Heavy

When it comes to programming, one of the biggest mistakes people make is trying to stick to a strength-focused (low rep/high weight) program through the holidays. Remember, this time of year has a way of throwing a monkey wrench into even the best-laid workout plans. Indeed, you might find that you don’t even have access to heavy enough weights to maintain your progress—let alone the time required to lift heavy properly.

“This is not the season where you’re going to set your PRs in the bench press or deadlift,” says Samuel. “This is a season to hit slightly higher reps so you don’t have to warm up as aggressively and give your body a little bit of a break while still training in attack mode.”

Use More Volume to Challenge Yourself

Samuel’s advice: Continue to lift like a beast, but instead of trying to max out at 4 to 6 reps per set, focus on completing 8 to 10 reps per set. This will work wherever you train (if you don't have access to your typical gym setup, for example) and you can still push your limits.

Don't Over-Focus on Cardio

For some reason, many people tend to hop off the strength train and hop on the cardio bus this time of year, says Samuel. Maybe you think it’s a more efficient way to sweat off the extra calories you’ve been eating and drinking, but the reality is that you’ll be much better off continuing to prioritize weight training.

“Don’t turn your workouts into a burpee-fest or treadmill session,” says Samuel. “Sure, it might burn some calories, but that’s not why we go to the gym, and it won’t help you maintain or advance your strength gains.”

Keep Up the Strength Work

What will keep the gains coming is continuing to push yourself to relative fatigue during your workouts and maximizing mechanical tension in your lifts.

“That’s not going to happen with burpees, and that’s not going to happen with cardio,” says Samuel, adding that prioritizing strength training and sticking to sets of 8 to 10 reps will help you be a lot more productive in your workouts during the holidays—even if they’re shorter and you’re not lifting as frequently.
