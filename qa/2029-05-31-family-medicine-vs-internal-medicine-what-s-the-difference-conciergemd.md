# Family Medicine vs. Internal Medicine: What’s the Difference? | ConciergeMD

07 Nov Family Medicine vs. Internal Medicine: What’s the Difference?

When considering a primary care provider, two common specialties come into focus: family medicine and internal medicine. Both are foundational to long-term health, prevention, and continuity of care, and both require rigorous medical training. However, they differ in the types of patients they treat and the breadth of their clinical scope.

Family medicine encompasses patients of all ages — from newborns to the elderly.

Internal medicine, often referred to as “adult medicine,” focuses exclusively on adults, particularly those with complex or chronic illnesses.

Understanding these differences can guide you in choosing a provider best suited to your needs.

What is Family Medicine?

Family medicine is a medical specialty in which physicians (family physicians) are trained to provide comprehensive, continuous care for patients of every age. [1] These clinicians often serve as the first point of contact in the healthcare system and coordinate care across specialties.

Family doctors combine preventive care with treatment for both acute and chronic conditions, managing everything from well-child visits to elder care.

What Family Medicine Includes

Family physicians typically provide:

Routine wellness visits, immunizations, and preventive health screenings.

Management of chronic illnesses like diabetes, hypertension, asthma, and more.

Pediatric and adolescent care, including growth, development, and immunizations.

This broad, whole-family medical model fosters long-term relationships and continuity of care.

What is Internal Medicine?

Internal medicine is a specialty dedicated to adults and the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of diseases that affect internal organs and systems. [2] Physicians in this field are called internists, and they often work in both outpatient (clinic) and inpatient (hospital) settings.

Similarities and Differences Between Family Medicine and Internal Medicine

Despite their differences, family medicine and internal medicine share several core characteristics:

Comprehensive primary care: Both serve as a patient’s main healthcare provider.

Prevention-focused: Lifestyle management, screening, and early detection are central to both specialties.
