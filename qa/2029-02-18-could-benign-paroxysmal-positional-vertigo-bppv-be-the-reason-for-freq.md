# Could Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) be the reason for frequent dizziness? | Ubie Doctor's Note

Could Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) be the reason for frequent dizziness?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a common inner ear disorder that can cause frequent, sudden episodes of dizziness. These vertigo spells are typically triggered by specific head movements or position changes, such as sitting up, lying down, rolling over in bed, or tilting your head to look up or down.

Episodes are usually brief, lasting less than a minute, but they can recur multiple times a day and may be accompanied by nausea, imbalance, or a spinning sensation. BPPV occurs when tiny calcium crystals in the inner ear become dislodged and disrupt your balance signals.

Because dizziness can stem from many causes—ranging from BPPV to other inner ear, neurological, or cardiovascular conditions—it's important to identify the source before assuming it's BPPV.
