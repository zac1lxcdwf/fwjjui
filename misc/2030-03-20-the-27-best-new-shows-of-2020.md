# The 27 Best New Shows of 2020

From 1999 on, viewers were lucky enough to be treated to what many referred to as "The Golden Age of Television." Shows like The Sopranos, Mad Men, Breaking Bad, and Game of Thrones brought a previously unheard of level of quality to the small screen, and things like Lost DVD boxsets and Stranger Things on Netflix taught people how to binge watch. Now, with so many different subscription services and cord-cutters, a new television era is among us, one with countless options at any given moment. But that doesn't mean there's any less quality content; in fact, quite the contrary. In 2020, our work was cut out for us as we tried to navigate the ever-expanding field and decide which shows are worth our precious time.

With so much madness going on in 2020—the COVID-19 pandemic, looking to escape election year anxiety, so on and so forth—we're usually just looking for that escape in the genre of our choice. Looking for horror? You're covered. Feel like a laugh? Gotcha. Are you just dying to see one of the best sports docs ever? Join the club. Shows like HBO's I May Destroy You might make you laugh and tear up within a single scene, and shows like Netflix's The Queen's Gambit will suddenly have you very invested in high stakes, ultra competitive chess tournaments. Shows like Apple TV+'s Ted Lasso will just make you laugh and feel good—and who doesn't need a little bit of that?

Familiar names like HBO and Netflix are joined now by newcomers like Disney+, Apple TV+, and who knows what else. Here are our choices for 2020's best new shows.

How To With John Wilson (HBO)

Sometimes the simplest shows are not only the most entertaining, but the smartest too. How To With John Wilson is a series of what are essentially short films, of a guy who uses his knowledge of life and knowledge of New York City to create a guide to understanding some relatively basic things. While the episodes may be titled 'Scaffolding' or 'Covering Furniture,' that's only the tip of the iceberg, as these oddball episodes eventually go to places where you truly do not expect them. This docu-comedy is best at your full attention, so make sure you lock your smartphone away in a cell for these six half hours—you won't regret it.

The Undoing (HBO)

If you thought "event" television ended with Game of Thrones, well, The Undoing may have just proven you wrong. This mystery gained enough traction through its six weeks on HBO that by the time the finale aired it felt like everyone was talking about it and wondering who did it. Nicole Kidman, Hugh Grant, Donald Sutherland, and Noah Jupe are all great in a series that will fill any Big Little Lies hole in your heart.

Ted Lasso (Apple TV+)

2020 was a garbage year where a lot of bad things happened. So that's why Ted Lasso—which stars Jason Sudeikis as a football-turned-soccer coach who first debuted in a series of 2013 ads—is in a lot of ways the show we needed most in 2020. A sports show with characters who you care about and episodes that just make you laugh and feel good, well, there's always a place for that. But especially this year. Especially this year.

Industry (HBO)

Imagine Euphoria combined with Wall Street but set in London and somehow possibly, like, ten times more dramatic. That's HBO's newest hit drama Industry, which looks at the lives of a handful of fresh graduates looking to get their footing in the ultra competitive—and frequently toxic—culture of finance and banking. This feels like a show that got a lot of buzz as its season went along (HBO dropped the last five episodes of the season on HBO Max as a surprise) but feels even more like a show that could get bigger and bigger with time.

The Flight Attendant (HBO Max)

Mystery lovers should look no farther than The Flight Attendant, which is a labor of love for lead Kaley Cuoco that also stars Game of Thrones' Michiel Huisman, Girls' Zosia Mamet, and the legendary Rosie Perez. Cuoco plays Cassie, a hard-partying flight attendant who finds herself in what can reasonably be called a very desperate situation. The show is light and pulpy in a way this genre typically is not, but great performances, interesting aesthetic choices, and a story that works more than it doesn't makes this show a winner.

Between The Flight Attendant and the Harley Quinn cartoon, Cuoco did a great job of getting the taste of The Big Bang Theory out of everyone's mouth.

The Queen's Gambit (Netflix)

The Queen's Gambit is a show about chess, which, admittedly, does not sound all that exciting. But trust us: this is probably the best show of the year. Anya Taylor-Joy stars as Beth Harmon, a young chess prodigy who battles drug addiction and finds various relationships in her life after losing her mother as a young child and ending up in an orphanage. Writer/director Scott Frank (who previously made Godless for Netflix) is a star here—every episode has a number of shots and moments that make you simply want to say "wow!" aloud. It's only a 7-episode limited series (meaning, don't expect a season 2), so that makes it not only great, but an easy show to zip through in a handful of sittings.

The Haunting of Bly Manor (Netflix)

The Haunting of Bly Manor is the follow-up to Netflix's 2018 hit The Haunting of Hill House; both shows are from creator Mike Flanagan (who has made such horror films as Doctor Sleep, Gerald's Game, and Hush in recent years), and feature much of the same cast. But the shows are actually quite different.

While Hill House was very much a character-driven and atmospheric horror (with some major scares included), Bly Manor tells a story that veers more toward gothic romance and drama, with some very excellent performances and visuals along the way. It's certainly scary, but the relationships almost seem to be the main attraction.

Loosely based on The Turn of the Screw by Henry James, the show tells a story that would be hard for anyone to forecast from the opening episode. The entire cast is excellent, but the standouts are Rahul Kohli, T'Nia Miller, and Victoria Pedretti, who not only was great in Hill House but was also exceptional in Season 2 of a very different Netflix drama—You.

The Good Lord Bird

Ethan Hawke leads this based-on-a-true-story limited series from famed horror production house Blumhouse, playing the real-life abolitionist John Brown that you may remember from high school history class. Get used to hearing this title, because Hawke goes big—he gets to really ham it up, but in an utterly watchable great way. The Good Lord Bird tells an interesting and relevant story of U.S. History, and the show and Hawke will almost certainly be major contenders at the Golden Globes and Emmys.

Also worth watching to see a great supporting turn from Daveed Diggs (as Frederick Douglass) and the de facto leading role from newcomer Joshua Caleb Johnson.

We Are Who We Are (HBO)

We Are Who We Are is the latest project of director Luca Guadagnino, who, in 2017, had the largest success yet in his lengthy career with the film Call Me By Your Name. Set in a similarly gorgeous Italian setting, We Are Who We Are tells a Euphoria-esque story about young people and identity. It's stunning to look at, has a banging soundtrack, and tells stories that would be of interest to anyone who's a fan of the 'teen dramedy' genre.

I May Destroy You (HBO)

Michaela Coel was already well-known for her series Chewing Gum, but her latest, HBO's I May Destroy You puts her talent on a whole new level of display. Coel, who also created, writes and directs the show, stars here as a young literary star named Arabella who's coming to terms with the world around her, which includes a not-so-committed long-distance boyfriend in Italy, some friends with problems of their own, and dealing with the aftermath of an absolutely chilling night out that ended with a blackout and a sexual assault. Based on Coel's own experience, I May Destroy You might be the year's closest thing to a must-watch show—it's one that could have you laughing, on the edge of your seat, and crying all within the same 30-minute episode.

The Outsider (HBO)

Based on Stephen King's novel of the same name, The Outisder stars Ben Mendelsohn and Cynthia Erivo as a pair of investigators with very different backgrounds looking for the solution to what would seem to be an unsolvable and heinous crime.

King called The Outsider one of the best adaptations of his work, and we'd be inclined to agree; the series finds a way to remain true to the creepy source material while also giving more depth to its characters and story.

High Fidelity (Hulu)

Remember the original High Fidelity? With John Cusack? And that legendary Jack Black dance? It's back, this time in an updated version set in modern-day Brooklyn, and starring Zoe Kravitz (Big Little Lies and future Catwoman in The Batman).

And—surprise surprise—this new version is fantastic. It touches on all the interesting threads that the film and novel do, but all in a very 2020 way. Kravitz is electric—almost too cool, in fact—in the show, and each 30-minute episode is a blast to watch. The soundtrack is bangin' too. Hulu won't be bringing High Fidelity back for a Season 2, which stinks. But for anyone looking for a good time, this is a breezy one that you won't regret spending the time on.

Mythic Quest (Apple TV+)

While Apple TV+ debuted their star-studden The Morning Show (with Jennifer Aniston, Reese Witherspoon, Billy Crudup, and Steve Carell) last year to moderate success, the new streaming service's best show so far is Mythic Quest, a video game comedy from Always Sunny star and creator Rob McElhenney. If you're into Silicon Valley with a bit of the Sunny humor, this show is an easy breeze that always makes you laugh. A stand-alone episode in the middle of the season might break your heart—and the next episode will have you laughing all over again. Plus, you can tell these guys are having fun making it; so much fun, in fact, that they got the whole gang back together in the midst of a pandemic to film a quarantine episode.

Devs (FX/Hulu)

Devs, which wrapped up its 8-episode run in April, is the latest high-concept sci-fi series from director Alex Garland (Ex Machina, Annihilation). With a cast led by Sonoya Mizuno and Nick Offerman, the show has stunning visuals and a compelling plot if you're willing to put in the effort to keep up. Seriously—make sure you're paying attention. The acting is exceptional, as is the writing. So lock your phone away, get focused, and give in to one of the best shows of 2020.

The Last Dance (ESPN)

With the absence of sports due to the COVID-19 pandemic, fans were so eager to see this 10-part documentary on Michael Jordan and the Chicago Bulls that its release was moved up by more than a month and a half (it was originally planned to air alongside the NBA Finals in June; instead it debuted in mid-April). With never-before-seen footage and interviews surrounding the legendary Michael Jordan-led Chicago Bulls dynasty (and an incredible soundtrack to match), the series has allowed sports fans of all ages to understand not only what Jordan meant to the world of basketball, but to the larger culture, too. As of mid-July, The Last Dance is on Netflix, too—so you really have no excuse.

I Know This Much Is True (HBO)

I Know This Much Is True is definitively not fun. In fact, it's a major downer—and, really, that's OK. Not everything has to be a feel-good story! As we're all seeing right now, sometimes life isn't fun, and I Know This Much Is True shows that as much as anything. Based on the Oprah-endorsed book by Wally Lamb and led by an absolutely incredible performance from Mark Ruffalo (playing twins, one of which is schizophrenic), this show is beautifully made, and each episode fleshes out different parts of different characters in the orbit of one of Ruffalo's characters, Dominick. Derek Cianfrance (who previously tackled bleak subject matter in his excellent films Blue Valentine and The Place Beyond The Pines) absolutely knocked this one out of the park. And we can't wait to see Ruffalo accept his Emmy.

The Third Day (HBO)

The Third Day might be the best thing for Midsommar fans to watch outside of Midsommar itself. This HBO limited series has two separate leads that shine in their own right; first Jude Law, who introduces viewers to the world of Osea Island and the inherent mystery waiting in the wings, and next Naomie Harris, who picks up where he leaves off. The show is part human drama, but is really at its best with it drifts into trippy horror territory—some images here are truly unsettling.

Perry Mason (HBO)

Perry Mason is the latest HBO's quasi-True Detective series (following Sharp Objects and The Outsider), and it might be the best of the bunch. A longtime passion project of Robert Downey Jr., the show features The Americans star Matthew Rhys as the titular character, here a dissheveled and frequently drunk private investigator, looking for information and tips about the particularly grisly death of a child. The mystery builds over the entire 8-episode season, and along the way the characters become just as interesting as the mystery itself. But that doesn't mean the ending isn't satisfying and thrilling in and of itself

The show—featuring a completely rebuilt version of 1920s Los Angeles—is gorgeous to look at, and Rhys is only one piece of a stellar cast that also includes great performances from Tatiana Maslany, Juliet Rylance, and a battering ram of incredible character actors, including Shea Whigham, Stephen Root, and John Lithgow.

Dave (FX/FX on Hulu)

Stick with us on this one. Dave is the origin story of satirical rapper Lil Dicky, real name Dave Burd. Not sold? Dave is also sort of like Atlanta, but set in basically our real world, with a ton of super famous and recognizable cameos. Still not sold? You might just have to check it out. Dave looks at the trials and tribulations in the career of Lil Dicky, here playing a fictional version of himself—and coming across very funny. Jeff Schaffer (The League, Curb Your Enthusiasm) is behind the show, which helps to explain why it's so particularly good. One key to Dave? The supporting characters are just as enjoyable and fleshed out as the main characters. It's only 10 half-hour episodes, so this is an easy, funny watch that you can get through in relatively short order.

Tiger King (Netflix)

Heard of this one? Tiger King took America by storm in late March, when it felt like everyone was watching the wild true story of Joe Exotic and all the larger-than-life (but somehow still very real) characters in his orbit. If you somehow missed it, what are you waiting for? Where some Netflix documentary series wind up a little thin due to lack of access (looking at you, Killer Inside: The Mind of Aaron Hernadnez), Tiger King is exactly the opposite. You see everything here. This documentary is expertly made, and an absolute f*cking trip.
