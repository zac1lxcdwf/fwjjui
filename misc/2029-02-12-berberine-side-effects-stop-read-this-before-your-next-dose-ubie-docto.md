Published on: 4/13/2026

Berberine Side Effects: Navigating Digestive Sensitivity and Safe Usage

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Berberine side effects are most commonly digestive: diarrhea, constipation, gas, bloating, cramping, and nausea. Less often, it can cause low blood sugar, low blood pressure, or headaches. Most effects are mild and dose-related, and improve by starting at a low dose, taking berberine with meals, splitting doses throughout the day, and monitoring blood sugar when relevant.

Use extra caution or avoid berberine if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, giving it to infants, have liver disease, or take insulin, diabetes drugs, blood pressure medications, blood thinners, immunosuppressants, certain antidepressants, or antibiotics. Seek urgent care for severe abdominal pain, ongoing diarrhea with dehydration, jaundice, fainting, chest pain, or trouble breathing.

Because berberine interacts with many medications and conditions, and symptoms can overlap with more serious issues, it's worth checking your specific symptoms before deciding what to do next. Explanation

Berberine is a plant compound found in herbs such as barberry, goldenseal, and Oregon grape. It has gained popularity for its potential benefits in supporting blood sugar balance, cholesterol levels, and metabolic health. While many people tolerate it well, berberine side effects are possible—especially involving the digestive system.

If you are considering berberine or already taking it, understanding its side effects can help you use it safely and confidently.

What Is Berberine?

Berberine is a naturally occurring alkaloid used in traditional Chinese and Ayurvedic medicine for centuries. Modern research suggests it may:

Support healthy blood sugar levels

Improve insulin sensitivity

Lower LDL ("bad") cholesterol and triglycerides

Support gut microbiome balance

Aid in weight management for some individuals

Despite these benefits, berberine is biologically active. That means it can affect multiple systems in the body—which is why side effects can occur.

Common Berberine Side Effects

The most frequently reported berberine side effects involve the gastrointestinal (GI) tract.

1. Digestive Upset

Digestive symptoms are the most common complaint, especially when starting berberine or taking higher doses.

You may experience:

Diarrhea

Constipation

Gas

Bloating

Abdominal cramping

Nausea

These symptoms typically happen because berberine affects gut bacteria and intestinal motility. In many cases, symptoms improve after the first few weeks as your body adjusts.

However, if you're experiencing persistent stomach pain or cramping, you can quickly check your symptoms with a free AI-powered tool to help determine whether your symptoms might require medical attention.

2. Changes in Bowel Movements

Berberine can alter gut motility and microbiome balance. Some people notice:

Looser stools

More frequent bowel movements

Temporary constipation

These changes are often dose-related. Starting with a lower dose and gradually increasing may reduce this risk.

3. Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycemia)

Because berberine can lower blood sugar, it may cause hypoglycemia in some individuals—especially those who:

Take insulin

Use other diabetes medications

Have naturally low blood sugar levels

Symptoms of low blood sugar include:

Dizziness

Shakiness

Sweating

Confusion

Rapid heartbeat

This is particularly important if you already manage diabetes. Combining berberine with prescription medications without medical supervision can increase risk.

Why Does Berberine Cause Digestive Side Effects?

Understanding why berberine side effects occur can make them less alarming.

Berberine works by:

Activating AMPK (an enzyme involved in metabolism)

Changing gut bacteria composition

Slowing carbohydrate absorption

Influencing intestinal movement

Because the gut is highly sensitive to change, these shifts can temporarily cause discomfort.

In fact, some researchers believe digestive side effects may partly reflect its activity in the gut microbiome. Still, discomfort should not be dismissed if it becomes intense or persistent.

Who Should Be Cautious with Berberine?

Certain individuals should use berberine carefully—or avoid it unless supervised by a physician.

1. Pregnant or Breastfeeding Individuals

Berberine may cross the placenta and has been associated with risks in newborns, particularly related to jaundice. It is generally not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

2. Infants and Newborns

Berberine is unsafe for newborns due to risk of kernicterus (a type of brain damage related to severe jaundice).

3. People Taking Prescription Medications

Berberine can interact with medications by affecting liver enzymes (particularly CYP450 enzymes) and P-glycoprotein transporters.

Possible interactions include:

Diabetes medications

Blood pressure drugs

Blood thinners

Immunosuppressants

Certain antidepressants

Antibiotics

If you take prescription medication, speak to a doctor before starting berberine.

4. People with Liver Disease

Although rare, liver enzyme changes have been reported. Those with liver conditions should use caution.

How to Reduce the Risk of Berberine Side Effects

If you and your healthcare provider decide berberine is appropriate, these strategies may reduce unwanted effects:

Start Low and Go Slow

Begin with 300–500 mg once daily.

Gradually increase to the commonly studied dose of 900–1500 mg per day (divided into 2–3 doses).

Take with Meals

This can reduce nausea and improve tolerance.

When to Stop Taking Berberine

Stop taking berberine and seek medical advice if you experience:

Severe or persistent abdominal pain

Ongoing diarrhea leading to dehydration

Symptoms of hypoglycemia

Significant dizziness or fainting

Any symptom that feels serious or unusual

Trust your instincts. Mild digestive discomfort can be common, but intense or worsening symptoms are not normal.
