SOMETIMES IT’S JUST too hot to wear hiking shoes. The Romans knew it, which is why their legions marched in sandals (where do you think gladiator sandals got their name?)

Popular among river guides and in mountain towns, hiking sandals are a staple of outdoor life. You’ll see them at trailheads, music festivals, and even on a thru-hiker or two. And for good reason: they’re lightweight, hot-weather-friendly, all-terrain vehicles with real performance capabilities. And the six pairs we recommend here are the best of the bunch.

What to Consider

Material and Fit

Hiking sandals have a range of materials and fit from recycled polyester to antimicrobial leather. Many have highly adjustable straps to accommodate different foot shapes. Think about your foot and your terrain needs, whether you’ll be in and out of water or on dry land. Keep in mind, everyone’s feet are a little different. While we’ve tried to mitigate this with a range of testers, it’s always a good idea to try before you buy.

Outsole

Other than a secure fit, grip is what differentiates a hiking sandal from a regular pair of flip-flops. When thinking about grip, consider two factors: tread and materials. Tread patterns and lug depth optimize a sandal’s grip for different terrains. And the outsole material is a game changer: look for tried-and-true names like Vibram (an ultra-grippy rubber) to really get the most performance out of each step.

Toe Protection

The sandals on this list are either open-toe (no toe protection) or closed-toe (some kind of toe protection that will shield from bumps and bruises). Closed-toe sandals like the Keen Newport 2 help on rocky terrain or riverbed hikes with unseen obstacles. Think about what terrain you plan to cover on your adventures. Closed-toe sandals breathe worse than their free and clear brethren, so also think about what you value more.

How Should Hiking Sandals Fit?

Hiking sandals need to be more secure than your normal beachcomber. That said, you don’t want them so tight your foot loses circulation. When hiking, you need to trust that your foot won’t slip. Take your normal size and play with the straps a bit—most hiking sandals are highly customizable. The goal is to find a middle ground between security and comfort, giving your foot some room to flex but not slip. And if you’re a half size, size up.

When Should I Hike in Sandals?

Short answer? Whenever you want! There are thru hikers who swear that sandals are the best for your feet over long distances. We’d steer you towards hiking shoes or boots in that scenario, but hiking with sandals generally makes sense when it’s warm and you need extra breathability and/or when you plan on water crossings. If you deal with gravel or pebbles, we recommend choosing a sandal with toe protection
