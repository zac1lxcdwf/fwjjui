# Michael Shannon Watching the Oscars Alone at a Bar Is the Apex of Manliness

Welcome to Manliest Moment, where we decide which dude crushed the subtle art of manliness the hardest this week. First up: Michael Shannon chooses alcohol over the Oscars.

Michael Shannon did not have time for the Oscars.

[Oscars Alone at Bar](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/)

While the rest of Hollywood was getting glitzed up for the industry’s biggest night, Michael Shannon threw on a puffy jacket he bought at Dick’s Sporting Goods for $12, tousled his hair into the style of a man who just completed an arduous woodland expedition, and watched Guillermo del Toro’s The Shape Of Water — a film Shannon starred in — win Best Picture with the sound turned off at Chicago’s Old Town Ale House.

"No sound on the TV, just sub-titles," the bar’s owner, Bruce Elliot, wrote on Twitter. "Of course the juke box was rocking, and the beer flowing. Where else would you want to spend Oscar night?"

What a blessed, manly image that is. If there were a Sistine Chapel of Being A Dude, that photo would be painted onto the ceiling, right next to a portrait of The Rock and his massive gorilla buddy kicking a giant crocodile in the dick.

A few things we’d especially like to highlight here:

1) The look of pure, unadulterated apathy on the man’s face. Michael Shannon put in an intense-as-balls performances in Shape of Water — spoiler alert: his character pulls off his own goddamn finger — and yet we can’t even tell if he’s happy his movie won, or he can’t quite read the list of $5 shots next to the TV.

2) His mustache, which we firmly believe he did not have when he walked into Old Town Ale House, and proceeded to grow over the course of the evening. (We like to think it started during Jimmy Kimmel’s opening monologue and reached its glorious peak as Jordan Peele won Best Screenplay for Get Out.)

3) If you casually walked into your local Chicago bar on a Sunday night and saw Michael Shannon in the corner with a half-full beer watching the Oscars on mute, would you approach him? Part of what makes him such an effective actor is his objectively frightening face. But you almost have to, right? Best case, one of Hollywood’s most talented performers appreciates your kind words. Worse case, one of Hollywood’s most talented performers smashes a glass over your head in a local Chicago bar, a story you can tell for years to come.
