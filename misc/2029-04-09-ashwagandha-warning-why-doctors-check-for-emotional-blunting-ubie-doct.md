Why Your Doctor Checks for Emotional Blunting (Anhedonia) on Ashwagandha

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Emotional blunting, also known as anhedonia, is a rare side effect that can occur with high-dose Ashwagandha use or when Ashwagandha is combined with other mood-altering medications or supplements. Doctors ask about changes in your ability to feel pleasure to help rule out underlying depression, identify possible drug interactions, and determine whether your dosage needs adjustment.

Key factors to consider include proper Ashwagandha dosing, potential supplement–medication interactions, and consistent mood monitoring. Recognizing warning signs early can help you and your doctor decide on the safest next steps.

Explanation

Ashwagandha is an adaptogenic herb used for centuries in Ayurvedic medicine. Today, many people turn to it to manage stress, boost energy, and support overall well-being. While Ashwagandha is generally well tolerated, some users report changes in mood—among them, emotional blunting or anhedonia (the inability to feel pleasure). Understanding why your doctor asks about these symptoms can help you use Ashwagandha safely and effectively.

What Is Emotional Blunting (Anhedonia)?

Emotional blunting, clinically known as anhedonia, describes a reduced ability to experience joy, excitement, or satisfaction. People with anhedonia may:

Feel numb, detached, or "flat"

Lose interest in hobbies, friends, or activities they once enjoyed

Describe life as "grey" or "meaningless"

While anhedonia is most often discussed in the context of depression or certain psychiatric medications, it can also be triggered or worsened by supplements, herbs, or changes in health status.

Ashwagandha and Mood: What We Know

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) contains active compounds called withanolides. These compounds interact with the body's stress response, helping to:

Lower cortisol (the "stress hormone")

Modulate GABA and serotonin pathways

Most studies and user reports emphasize benefits like reduced anxiety, improved sleep, and enhanced endurance. However, no supplement is entirely free of side effects, and a small number of people may notice mood shifts.

Common Side Effects of Ashwagandha

Based on clinical trials and user surveys, side effects tend to be mild:

Gastrointestinal upset (nausea, diarrhea)

Headaches

Sleepiness or dizziness (especially at higher doses)

Reports of Emotional Blunting

Although rare, there are anecdotal cases where individuals on high-dose Ashwagandha or taking it alongside other mood-altering supplements/medications describe:

Feeling "emotionally distant"

Reduced intensity of both positive and negative feelings

Difficulty "getting excited" about things

These experiences can overlap with anhedonia and are why doctors will specifically check for emotional blunting on Ashwagandha.

Why Your Doctor Asks About Anhedonia

When you mention using Ashwagandha, your healthcare provider may ask targeted questions about your mood and emotional range. Here's why:

Differentiate Supplement Effects vs. Underlying Condition

Anhedonia can signal untreated or worsening depression, bipolar disorder, or other mental health issues.

Your doctor wants to know if the herb is unmasking or exacerbating a pre-existing problem.

Rule Out Drug Interactions

Ashwagandha may interact with antidepressants, benzodiazepines, or other medications that influence mood.

Combined effects on GABA or serotonin systems can sometimes lead to emotional dulling.

Assess Dosage and Duration

High doses (exceeding 600–1,000 mg of standardized extract per day) can increase the risk of side effects.

Your doctor may recommend tapering the dose to see if mood symptoms improve.

Monitor Comprehensive Mental Health

Tracking changes in joy, motivation, and pleasure helps your provider build a complete picture of your well-being.

Emotional blunting, if unaddressed, can affect relationships, work, and overall quality of life.

Signs to Watch For

If you're taking Ashwagandha, be alert to any persistent shifts in how you feel emotionally. Key warning signs include:

Loss of interest in previously enjoyable activities

Feeling detached from friends or loved ones

A general sense of emptiness or boredom

Difficulty feeling pleasure, even when good things happen

Should you notice these changes, jot down when they started, how severe they are, and whether they correlate with changes in your Ashwagandha dose or other medications.

What You Can Do

Track Your Symptoms

Keep a simple mood journal (date, rating of happiness on a 1–10 scale, notes on events).

Note any changes after starting, stopping, or adjusting Ashwagandha.

Review Other Medications or Supplements

List everything you take, including over-the-counter products and herbs.

Look for combinations that might amplify calming or sedating effects.

Balancing Benefits and Risks

Ashwagandha offers proven benefits for many people, but like any supplement, it isn't risk-free. Your doctor's questions about anhedonia aren't meant to alarm you—they're a routine way to keep track of how you're responding, physically and emotionally.

Tips for Safe Use

Start with a low dose (e.g., 300–500 mg of a standardized extract once daily).

Take it with meals to reduce gastrointestinal side effects.

Avoid combining high doses of Ashwagandha with other sedatives or mood-altering drugs unless supervised by a physician.

Be patient: adaptogens often take 4–6 weeks to show full effects.

When to Seek Immediate Help

While mild anhedonia can be monitored over a few weeks, contact your healthcare provider right away if you experience:

Thoughts of harming yourself or others

Severe emotional numbness interfering with daily life

Sudden mood swings combined with other concerning symptoms (e.g., chest pain, fainting)

If you or someone else is in danger, seek emergency care immediately.
