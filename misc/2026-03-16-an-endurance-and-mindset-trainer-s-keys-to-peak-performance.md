# An Endurance and Mindset Trainer's Keys to Peak Performance

[Keys to Peak Performance](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/blog/6062068136.html)

The fitness industry was hit particularly hard by the COVID-19 pandemic. Gyms and studios spent most of the year shuttered, and while many are finally reopening, progress has been slow and and major restrictions remain in place. In addition to training athletes from all around the world (he was early to the virtual party), endurance coach Sam Tooley owns two of those gyms—Alpha Fit Club and Alpha Performance Studio, both in his hometown of Westfield, New Jersey—but closing them hasn't left him any less busy.

In fact, Tooley hustles as much as ever these days, he's just had to approach things a little differently. And he's uniquely suited to adapt to unprecedented times, thanks to his other coaching specialty: mindset.

“My routine had been so contingent upon getting up at 3:30, 3:45 in the morning and going over to the gym,” he says. “[The pandemic] has allowed me to double down on stuff I was maybe neglecting before and focus on areas of the business I didn't have time to improve upon.”

Beyond maintaining his own training along with his brick-and-mortar businesses, Tooley has continued coaching athletes virtually with his speed and power endurance training program Project Alpha. So it’s safe to say the man knows the importance of having a routine and sticking to it. Here are a few of Tooley's favorite tips for keeping your routine intact regardless of outside chaos.

Lean on community for accountability

The pandemic wrecked pretty much everyone’s regular routine, so Tooley took his love of fitness to engage with the community and keep everyone healthy—and sane. Tooley attributes much of the growth and success of his two gyms to their roots in his home town, so motivating that community through uncertain times became a way for him to pay it forward.

“Whether that was initially IGTV workouts and Zoom to now doing outdoor workouts as much as we can, I’m just trying to stay front of mind for them,” Tooley says.

Pushing that energy and love of fitness outward has given Tooley extra purpose during a tough time. When you engage with your community, all sorts of motivation can follow.

“Just checking in on friends and making sure that they are getting out and moving—it’s been crucial,” Tooley says. “I have certainly leaned on buddies, other trainers, and things like that to hold myself accountable.”

Write it out

You’d be surprised what keeping a logged account of your day can do for your motivation.

“I got sober four years ago,” Tooley says, “and part of that process was journaling. Getting my thoughts on paper has always been a big outlet.”

Journaling helps keep your mind sharp and focused; clarify your own feelings, doubts, and struggles; and chart your progress.

“When you write something down, it's almost like you’re saying it out loud,” Tooley says, “and that allows you to see the truth of the matter more so than if you just leave it to jumble around in your head.”

Give your diet a boost

Meal consistency is key when you’re trying to get in shape, and Tooley eats pretty much the same meals each day: black coffee and a post-workout shake for breakfast, rice and veggies with a helping of chicken or another protein for lunch and dinner.

That may come across as slightly boring, or perhaps lacking in protein to some, but Tooley augments his diet with EPIC’s all new beef bars. The keto-friendly bars are made with 100 percent grass-fed beef and spices, and are a good source of protein that is satisfying but not too filling to slow down Tooley’s next workout.

“I think at first glance you assume a snack with 10 or 11 grams of protein will be heavy, but they’re very light,” Tooley says. “It’s a nice snack at midday or between meals to make sure I don’t get hangry, especially since I’ve been up since 4:00 in the morning.

Set your intentions

It’s easy to just get up, get to your routine, and go through the motions. Tooley suggests something a little different.

“I still get up at 4:00 A.M. every day,” he says. “One thing I do is start slow to go fast. I get really clear about the intentions I have for the day. How do I want to operate? How do I want to carry myself? How do I want to treat other people?”

“I run through a few questions, and that sets the tone for the day because once I do get going, I’m at it. I’m moving from 5:00 A.M. until 8:00 or 9:00 P.M., and that’s every day.”

It can be tempting to check out and go through the motions, but Tooley finds it’s the extra intentions in the morning that takes him the extra mile.
