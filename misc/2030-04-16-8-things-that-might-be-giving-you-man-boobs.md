# 8 Things That Might Be Giving You Man Boobs

Man boobs might sound like a joke, but for the more than 30 percent of guys who deal with enlarging breasts at some point or another during their lives, it’s no laughing matter.

And we're not talking about the kind of man boobs you get when you pack on some extra pounds. Yes, just like the rest of your body, you can store fat in your breasts, and that can come and go with weight fluctuations. But many causes of actual breast development in guys aren't so simple.

When you develop glandular tissue in your breasts—as opposed to fat tissue—it's called gynecomastia. The culprits behind full-fledged man boobs largely have to do with your hormones.

"The [development] of extra breast tissue occurs when you have relatively lower testosterone levels or relatively higher estrogen levels," explains endocrinologist Bradley Anawalt, M.D., chief of medicine at the University of Washington Medical Center. Put simply, testosterone suppresses breast growth, while too much estrogen can encourage it (Here are 8 sneaky signs your testosterone is too low).

But at worst, it can also be a sign of more serious health problems. Here are eight causes of man boobs to know about, and what you can do about it.

Cause of Man Boobs: Obesity

There's more to obesity-related gynecomastia than just increased fat all over. In fact, that increased fat is what disrupts your hormones. Your fat tissue produces a hormone called leptin, which plays an important role in reproductive function, explains Dr. Anawalt.

If you don't have enough fat tissue, your leptin levels will be very low. But in the case of obesity, leptin levels are very high, which can lead to leptin resistance—which tricks your brain into just thinking it's starving.

In both cases, "this sends a signal to the brain that this is an unsuitable time to waste energy on sex and having kids," explains Dr. Anawalt. This translates to a decrease in the hormones LH and FSH, which can stunt sperm and testosterone production.

"Testosterone causes decreased growth of the cells that cause the glandular tissue [in breasts], and estrogen increases it," says Dr. Anawalt. Since obesity lowers testosterone, you're getting less of its "suppression" effect on breast tissue, so estrogen can take over and cause development of, well, man boobs. Cause of Man Boobs: Testosterone Replacement Therapy

While testosterone treatment can help improve your sex life, muscle mass, energy, mood, and more, it can also cause some temporary breast growth. This is because all men have an enzyme called aromatase that converts testosterone to a form of estrogen, says Dr. Anawalt.

"So if you start taking testosterone, testosterone levels go up, but in addition, estrogen levels go up because you're converting some testosterone to estrogen,” he says. “That small increase in estrogen is a big percentage increase, so when you first get started, you can get a little bit of breast tenderness and growth.”

The good news is, this isn't usually noticeable to other people, and in most cases, it recedes within a few weeks. "The anti-breast growth effects of testosterone begin to prevail, because your testosterone levels are sky high compared to where you were before," says Dr. Anawalt.

If it doesn't go away (which can happen in very rare cases), talk to your doctor. They may stop treatment for a month or two to allow breast tissue to return to normal (Here's what to expect when you start testosterone therapy).

Cause of Man Boobs: Steroids

Illegal testosterone treatments used for bulking up or enhancing performance can also cause breast growth—we're talking anabolic steroids, which are synthetic versions of testosterone.

Some of these don't get converted to estrogen, but the ones that do can cause breast growth in the same way that legit testosterone treatment can.

And taking a drug to block the conversion of testosterone to estrogen also isn't a good idea, says Dr. Anawalt. Estrogen is still important for bone health, sexual function, and keeping fat mass down in men, so you don't want to block it entirely.

It's also worth noting that the side effects with steroids are shadier than with regular testosterone treatment. "With performance-enhancing drugs, you don't really know what it is and how much you should use," says Dr. Pensler, so guys often end up taking way more than they should, which can lead to more pronounced effects.

Cause of Man Boobs: Plant Oils and Natural Grooming Products

While "all natural" grooming products may seem harmless, there are certain plant oil ingredients that have estrogen-like properties. The most commonly cited example is lavender oil, says Dr. Anawalt. Research has shown that it can cause gynecomastia, so he doesn't recommend using it extensively. "There's also some speculation about tea tree oils, but that's not been proven," says Dr. Anawalt.

You don't necessarily have to be using it on your chest to see breast growth effect.

"If you're using a lot of lavender oil all over the body, it's going to get into your bloodstream and make it to the breasts," he says. Although applying directly to your chest can cause a local effect, too.

While lavender oil has been linked to gynecomastia, other oils may also play a role, too. "If you start developing some breast tenderness and you've just started using natural oils in the past three to six months, you should think about that as a potential culprit and stop the oil," says Dr. Anawalt.

Cause of Man Boobs: Hair Loss Drugs and Other Medications

There's a long list of medications that list gynecomastia as a potential rare side effect, but some are more strongly linked than others. Notably, a hair loss drug called Propecia (finasteride) has been associated with gynecomastia.

"Anti-hair loss drugs cause a decrease in the conversion of testosterone to a really powerful form of testosterone, called dihydrotestosterone, or DHT. So you're having an anti-testosterone effect," says Dr. Anawalt.

Other drugs can block testosterone receptors to create this anti-testosterone effect, like spironalactone, a diuretic used in people with liver disease, and heartburn meds like Tagamet (cimetidine), says Dr. Anawalt.

It's important to remember that while gynecomastia is listed as a rare side effect of countless medications, a cause and effect relationship isn't always clear—while the meds above do play a role in gynecomastia, with others, a relationship is less clear.

Cause of Man Boobs: Severe Liver or Kidney Disease

Any serious illness can cause a decrease in testosterone production, because your brain gets the message that it's not a good time to reproduce. But severe liver disease, such as cirrhosis, is particularly tied to hormone disruption.

"Liver disease tends to cause disruption in protein synthesis, and certain proteins get over-produced," says Dr. Anawalt. "One of them is a protein called sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG), which binds testosterone." So not only is testosterone production decreased due to severe illness, but there's also an increase in the protein that binds testosterone, so there's less "free" testosterone in the body to do its job.

"These men with liver disease get a double whammy," says Dr. Anawalt.

Kidney disease can also have this two-fold effect, but you'd have to have pretty profound kidney disease to notice this, says Dr. Anawalt. Far more common symptoms—like urinary changes, swelling of your ankles or feet, skin rash, back or side pain, or nausea and vomiting, for instance—would present themselves before you noticed man boobs.

Cause of Man Boobs: Undetected Tumors

Several types of tumors, like testicular tumors and pituitary tumors, can impact hormone production. If your body isn’t making enough testosterone, or is making too much estrogen, this ratio imbalance can lead to gynecomastia. "The other tumor effect that can happen with some lung tumors and testicular tumors is to make a hormone called HCG," explains Dr. Anawalt. "What HCG does is it acts like the hormone LH to stimulate testosterone production from the testicles, but it also stimulates more conversion of testosterone to estrogen."

You also might've heard of HCG because it's often used as a "natural" way to increase testosterone or lose weight, but HCG injections also have this effect on estrogen production, which can cause breast growth.

Cause of Man Boobs: You’re Getting Older

Gynecomastia can occur at any age, but it often happens first during puberty, when your hormones are all over the place, says Dr. Pensler.

"All males' breasts undergo some development, so every male will have some thickening of tissues. The overwhelming majority has resolution with time—it goes away,” he says. “But some people it doesn't, and it just sits there," he says.

In this case, it's called idiopathic gynecomastia. If you've had man boobs all your life, this is likely the case, he says.

On the other end of the spectrum, gynecomastia becomes more likely as you get older.

Lowering testosterone levels may play a role, says Dr. Anawalt, but part of it is just normal aging.

"Over our lifetimes, we're probably exposed to various conditions where our testosterone levels drop because of severe illness, or we've been exposed to some estrogens in an environment that we don't know about," says Dr. Anawalt. "There are all sorts of things that probably contribute, so we wind up shrugging our shoulders and saying, 'this is probably normal.'"

When To See Your Doctor About Man Boobs

If you're experiencing unexplained man boobs, it's important to see a doctor early to investigate any underlying causes that need to be treated—especially if there are other symptoms present.

Plus, the earlier you identify what's causing your gynecomastia, the more likely it'll resolve with treatment. Dr. Anawalt says you should get it checked out during the first six months you notice it, particularly when there's breast tenderness, which suggests active growth and recent onset.

"At the point that the breast tissue has been there for more than a year or so and there's no longer breast tenderness, the glandular breast tissue actually turns into fibrosis, like a scar, and that will not go away by itself," says Dr. Anawalt. In this case, it'll need to be surgically removed.

If you do see a surgeon, he or she will also do a full work-up and take a medical history to make sure there's nothing else going on. If you don't address an underlying cause, the breast tissue will come back (Here's more on the cure for man boobs).

"The people who undergo the surgery have such an important improvement in their self-worth and the quality of life that they're able to lead," says Dr. Pensler. So ultimately, if your man boobs bother you and there's no active underlying cause, getting them taken care of might be worthwhile.
