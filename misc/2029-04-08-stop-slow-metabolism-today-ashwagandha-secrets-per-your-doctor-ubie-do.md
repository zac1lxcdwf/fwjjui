Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is a staple herb in Ayurvedic medicine, often called "Indian ginseng" or "winter cherry." It's classified as an adaptogen—meaning it helps your body adapt to physical and mental stress. Traditional uses include:

Improving sleep quality

Enhancing strength and stamina

Modern research has taken a closer look at its impact on hormones, body composition and metabolic rate.

3. Key Mechanisms: How Ashwagandha and Metabolic Speed Are Linked

Scientific studies reveal several ways Ashwagandha may boost metabolic speed:

Cortisol reduction• Chronic stress raises cortisol, which promotes fat storage—especially around the belly.• Ashwagandha has been shown to significantly lower cortisol levels in human trials, easing that stress-fat cycle.

Thyroid support• Your thyroid gland produces hormones (T3 and T4) that regulate basal metabolic rate (BMR).• Animal and preliminary human studies suggest Ashwagandha can enhance thyroid hormone levels, helping your body burn calories more efficiently.

Improved muscle mass• Lean muscle tissue burns more calories at rest than fat.• Supplementing with Ashwagandha alongside resistance training led to greater gains in strength and muscle mass, according to a study in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition.

Enhanced mitochondrial function• Mitochondria are the "power plants" of your cells.• Ashwagandha's antioxidant properties may protect mitochondrial membranes, boosting energy production at the cellular level.

Reduced inflammation• Chronic inflammation can impair metabolic health.• By tamping down inflammatory markers, Ashwagandha may help restore a more efficient metabolic environment.

While more large-scale trials are needed, current research is promising:

Cortisol and Stress Study (2008)In a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, adults under chronic stress who took 300 mg of Ashwagandha extract twice daily reported a 27.9% reduction in cortisol levels versus placebo (source: Indian Journal of Psychological Medicine).

Muscle Gain and Strength Study (2015)Healthy men aged 18–50 who took 300 mg of Ashwagandha extract twice daily for 8 weeks alongside weight training gained significantly more muscle and strength than the placebo group. Resting energy expenditure also rose in the Ashwagandha group (source: Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition).

Thyroid Function Pilot Study (2018)In a small open-label study, subclinical hypothyroid patients saw improvements in T3 and T4 hormone levels after 8 weeks of Ashwagandha supplementation (source: Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine).

Body Composition in Overweight AdultsA randomized trial reported modest—but meaningful—reductions in body fat percentage and waist circumference when overweight participants took standardized Ashwagandha extract for 12 weeks compared to placebo (source: Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine).
