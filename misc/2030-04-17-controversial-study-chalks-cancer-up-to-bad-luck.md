# Controversial Study Chalks Cancer Up to Bad Luck

A controversial new study from the Johns Hopkins Kimmel Cancer Center suggests that most cancer-causing mutations are simply unavoidable and random.

[Cancer Up](https://protocols.peptidebiotechhgh.com/article/ageineer-peptide-protocol-reverse-understanding-ageineer-peptide-protocol-reverse-core-views-of-peptide-academic-research-updates-peptide-share-b68f44737173/)

Bert Vogelstein and Cristian Tomasetti have doubled down on their much-debated report of similar findings two years ago, according to NPR. They still believe cancer can be caused by environmental factors, such as smoking, or genetics—but Vogelstein said their findings suggest only 40 percent of cancers are actually avoidable.

Vogelstein explained at a press conference that every time a cell divides, it makes several mistakes when copying its DNA, most of them insignificant. “But occasionally they occur in a cancer driver gene,” Vogelstein said. “That’s bad luck.”

Only two or three of these driver genes need mutate in the same cell to create a cancer cell. And that 40 percent figure above: that’s a generous quote. The new study in Science Magazine reports 66 percent of mutations are completely random, 29 percent are due to environmental factors, and only 5 percent are from genetics.
