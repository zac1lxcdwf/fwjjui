How does Chikungunya virus spread to humans?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Chikungunya virus is primarily transmitted to humans through the bite of infected mosquitoes, most commonly Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus species. These mosquitoes typically bite during daytime hours and thrive in tropical and subtropical regions. Less commonly, the virus can spread from mother to newborn during childbirth or through exposure to infected blood.

Explanation

Chikungunya virus is primarily spread to humans by mosquito bites. The main culprits are Aedes mosquitoes, specifically Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus. These mosquitoes become infected when they bite a person already carrying the virus. Once the mosquitoes are infected, they can spread the virus to other people through their bites.

Key Points:

Mosquito Vectors: Aedes mosquitoes are the main vectors, meaning they are the ones that carry and spread the virus.

Infection Cycle: When a mosquito bites an infected person, it can pick up the virus. After a few days, the mosquito can then transmit the virus to another person through a bite.

Global Spread: The virus has spread to many parts of the world, including Africa, Asia, and the Americas, largely due to the widespread presence of Aedes mosquitoes in these regions.
