Does prednisolone weaken the immune system?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Yes, prednisolone can weaken the immune system. As a corticosteroid, it suppresses immune activity by reducing inflammation and lowering the body's response to infections, foreign substances, and even its own tissues. This makes it effective for treating autoimmune conditions, allergies, and inflammatory diseases, but it also increases the risk of infections, especially with long-term or high-dose use.

Common effects on immunity include:

Reduced white blood cell activity

Slower wound healing

Higher susceptibility to viral, bacterial, and fungal infections

Weakened response to vaccines

Explanation

Prednisolone is a type of medicine called a corticosteroid, which can affect the immune system. It is used to reduce inflammation and treat various conditions, but it also has the effect of weakening the immune system.

How Prednisolone Affects the Immune System:

Reduces Immune Response: Prednisolone can make the immune system less active. This means the body might not fight off infections as well as it usually does.

Impact on Skin Tests: Studies show that prednisolone can affect skin tests that check for immune reactions, like the tuberculin test used for tuberculosis.

Innate and Adaptive Immunity: Prednisolone can affect both the immediate (innate) and long-term (adaptive) parts of the immune system, as seen in research with children and adults.
