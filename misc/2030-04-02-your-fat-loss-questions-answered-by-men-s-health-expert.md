Q: How much weight should I use for each exercise?

Answer:

This is one of my most popular reader mail questions. Unfortunately, I can't give everyone a precise answer because it depends on so many factors.

But for absolute beginners, here's what you need to do...and it's very simple.

Start with the lowest amount of weight possible. If you are doing dumbbell rows, start with the 5 lbs dumbbell. Use perfect form. If you can do all of the repetitions without any struggle, then increase the weight on the next set.

By simply using trial and error, you'll find the correct weight quite quickly.

Start conservative, and don't rush.

Q: For my intervals, I'm running at 7.5mph on the treadmill, and then doing the recovery interval at 6.0. Is that the right way to do it?

Answer:Great work on the running. Working at 7.5 is a serious fat burning interval speed.

However, I want you to cut back the recovery speed...all the way back down to a walking speed of 3.5mph.

Remember - the main goal of recovery is full recovery. If you run at 6mph during the recovery interval, you are simply doing aerobic training...not a Turbulence Training Interval for Fat Loss.

It doesn't matter if you run at 5.5 or 9.9mph during your "work" interval, you must always bring it down to a slow walk during your recovery interval.

Adjust your interval workouts accordingly if you ride the bike or row or use bodyweight exercises.

Q: I have an obese client (300 pounds) that needs to lose a lot of weight. How many calories should he eat per day?

Answer:At this weight, the calorie calculator recommendations (found in TT for Fat Loss) can't be used. Otherwise, you would over-estimate calorie and protein intake.

The first thing to do is to take a look at his current calorie and food intake. Use fitday.com. That will help you remove the worst foods and replace them with some healthy options.

(But don't change everything at once, that could upset his stomach. Make one change per day.)

Next, we generally cut the calories back to no more than 2500 per day (unless he has a lot of muscle). Also, 200g of protein is likely to be enough. Take care of the protein choices first, then the fruits and vegetables, the healthy fats, and then additional healthy carbohydrate choices spread throughout the day - but stick them close to workouts whenever possible.
