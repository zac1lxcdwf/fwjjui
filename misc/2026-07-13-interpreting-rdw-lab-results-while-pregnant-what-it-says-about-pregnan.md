Interpreting RDW Lab Results While Pregnant: What It Says About Pregnancy Anemia

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

RDW in pregnancy measures the variation in your red blood cell size and is a key indicator for identifying anemia. While a high RDW commonly signals iron deficiency anemia, accurate interpretation requires reviewing it alongside hemoglobin, hematocrit, MCV, and iron studies to pinpoint the true cause. Specific RDW patterns can distinguish between iron, folate, or B12 deficiencies, and even a normal RDW paired with low hemoglobin has important clinical meaning.

The good news: most pregnancy-related anemia is highly treatable with targeted supplements and dietary changes, and RDW is a useful tool for monitoring your response to treatment. Recognizing red flag symptoms—such as extreme fatigue, shortness of breath, or dizziness—and knowing when to follow up with your clinician is essential for you and your baby's health.

What Is RDW?

RDW stands for Red Cell Distribution Width. It is part of a standard complete blood count (CBC), a routine test commonly done during pregnancy.

The rdw blood test measures how much your red blood cells vary in size.

If most red blood cells are about the same size → RDW is normal.

If there is a wide mix of small and large red blood cells → RDW is elevated.

Red blood cells carry oxygen to your body and your baby. When something interferes with healthy red blood cell production, the size of the cells often becomes more variable — and RDW increases.

Why Is RDW Important During Pregnancy?

Pregnancy naturally changes your blood.

Blood volume increases.

Iron needs increase.

Your body must produce more red blood cells to support the placenta and baby.

Because of these changes, anemia is common in pregnancy. The World Health Organization reports that iron deficiency anemia is one of the most frequent medical conditions during pregnancy worldwide.

The rdw blood test helps your provider determine:

Whether anemia may be present

What type of anemia you might have

Whether iron deficiency is likely

How your body is responding to treatment

RDW alone does not diagnose anemia — it must be interpreted alongside:

Hemoglobin

Hematocrit

MCV (Mean Corpuscular Volume)

Iron studies (if needed)

What Is a Normal RDW in Pregnancy?

Typical RDW reference ranges for adults are:

11% to 15%

However, ranges can vary slightly depending on the lab.

Pregnancy itself does not dramatically change RDW values, but mild variation can occur as your body adapts to increased blood production.

Your doctor will interpret your result in the context of your full blood panel.

What Does a High RDW Mean During Pregnancy?

An elevated RDW means your red blood cells vary more in size than expected. In pregnancy, this most commonly suggests:

1. Iron Deficiency Anemia (Most Common Cause)

Iron deficiency is the leading cause of anemia during pregnancy.

Why it happens:

Increased iron demand from the growing baby

Increased blood volume

Not enough iron intake from diet

With iron deficiency:

Red blood cells are often smaller than normal (low MCV)

RDW increases because newer cells differ in size from older ones

This is usually very treatable with iron supplementation and dietary changes.

2. Vitamin Deficiencies

Less commonly, high RDW can be caused by:

Vitamin B12 deficiency

Folate deficiency

These deficiencies may cause larger red blood cells (high MCV) along with elevated RDW.

Folate is especially important in pregnancy for preventing neural tube defects, which is why prenatal vitamins contain folic acid.

What If RDW Is Normal but Hemoglobin Is Low?

A normal rdw blood test does not rule out anemia.

For example:

Anemia of chronic disease may have normal RDW.

Dilutional anemia of pregnancy (from increased plasma volume) may not elevate RDW.

That's why your provider looks at the entire CBC — not just one number.

Is High RDW Dangerous During Pregnancy?

An elevated RDW itself is not dangerous. It is a marker — not a disease.

The concern is the underlying cause.

Untreated moderate to severe anemia during pregnancy can increase risks such as:

Preterm delivery

Low birth weight

Maternal fatigue and decreased ability to tolerate blood loss during delivery

However, most cases of pregnancy-related anemia are mild and respond well to treatment.

The key is early detection and proper management.

How Is Pregnancy Anemia Treated?

Treatment depends on the cause.

If Iron Deficiency Is Confirmed

Your provider may recommend:

Oral iron supplements

Taking iron with vitamin C to improve absorption

Avoiding calcium at the same time as iron

Increasing iron-rich foods

Can RDW Be Used to Monitor Treatment?

Yes.

As your body begins producing healthy red blood cells, RDW may temporarily rise even more before normalizing. This happens because:

Older abnormal cells are still circulating

New healthy cells are being produced

Over time, RDW should stabilize as red blood cell production becomes uniform again.

Your doctor may repeat your CBC to monitor progress.

RDW is a clue — not a diagnosis.
