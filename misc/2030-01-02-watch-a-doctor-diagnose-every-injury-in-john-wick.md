# Watch a Doctor Diagnose Every Injury in ‘John Wick’

[Injury in John](https://antimicrobial-hub.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/9431206043.html)

YouTube's Doctor Mike, a.k.a. Mike Varshavski MD, has been making content throughout the pandemic that dispels medical misinformation and helps people make more informed decisions about their own health. And when he's not doing that, he watches TV and movies to gage their medical accuracy. In a recent video, Mike sits down to watch John Wick, and diagnoses every single injury that occurs throughout the action-packed revenge flick's 101-minute runtime.

"I feel like he's going to get concussed a lot here," he says, watching the scene in which Keanu Reeves is attacked at home. "If you're getting hit with a solid object on the back of your skull, that's going to cause a very serious injury. Not only is it going to split your skull open and you're going to start bleeding a lot, but you're also going to get your brain rattled."

[Watch Doctor Diagnose Every](https://cellular.wiki/2lizs8/)

The same scene also sees John Wick get winded after being hit right in the solar plexus, and sustaining a broken humerus and broken nose. This, says Mike, is where the first glaring inaccuracy pops up (the villains' pigeon Russian notwithstanding).

"The fact that his nose stopped bleeding here is also not real. Like, what made his nose stop bleeding?" He says. "It would actually become a problem, because he's laying on his back, it's going to go right into his oral cavity, he's going to choke on it, if he swallows it it's going to cause gastrointestinal disturbances, he's going to have diarrhea later on."

He also explains that while windows always shatter when somebody is thrown through them in the movies, in real life they actually break into larger, incredibly sharp shards which can do a lot of damage, such as severing an artery.

One of the most painful-looking injuries in the film occurs when John Wick stabs somebody through their lower jaw. "You have got so many nerve fibers in your mouth, in your tongue, this is why kissing feels good," explains Mike. "It's why you can always feel a little blister in your mouth. That would just... I would scream out."

The worst injury, though, according to Doctor Mike at least, belongs to the guy who gets thrown off a balcony. "There's a vertebral fracture, there might even be a hip fracture depending on how he landed on his pelvis," he says. And by "worst" injury, of course, he is excluding all of the many, many bad guys who get shot and killed throughout the movie, for whom a trip to the E.R. would be too little too late.
