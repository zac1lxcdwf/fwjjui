# The 20 Most Terrifying Horror Video Games Ever

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

The funny thing about horror video games is that some of the best ones ever made were designed to discourage you from playing them. As developers realized they had the power to throw gamers into the most horrible situations imaginable and let them figure out how to escape them (if escape was ever even possible), we went through a kind of “arms race” in which the best designers in the world competed to see who could scare us the most. At the height of that movement, the winner was a multi-way tie between, well, pretty much all of them.

Today, the horror gaming scene is a little bit different. If you want to find those games that will scare you past the point of reason and leave you questioning what you were thinking when you decided to spend your evening playing them, you will find them. These days, though, a new generation of horror game creators have explored the possibilities of new concepts such as multiplayer horror, VR horror, and even just horror games that you’ll actually have fun playing because they let you explore these worlds for a few hours. In fact, the only thing that each of the best modern horror games have in common is that they not only represent some of the best experiences you’ll find in the horror genre regardless of where you seek out your scares, but they’re some of the best games you’ll ever play, regardless of their genre.

Here are 20 of the best horror video games available now:

Carrion

If you’ve ever watched The Thing or The Blob and secretly wondered what it would be like to be the creature rather than the survivors, you’ll be happy to hear that Carrion is the one game that will let you live your darkest horror fantasies. Described as a “reverse-horror” game, Carrion casts you as a monster that must escape the massive laboratory it’s being studied in. The game’s surprisingly strong story carries a lot of emotional weight, but the real draw here is the chance to wreak untold havoc as a nearly unstoppable creature of pure horror that only grows as it consumes anyone who stands in its way.

The Dark Pictures Anthology: Man of Medan

Just as they did with their breakout horror hit, Until Dawn, developer Supermassive Games uses Man of Medan as an excuse to pay tribute to their favorite horror tropes and B-movie classics. Unlike Until Dawn, Man of Medan features a brilliant multiplayer mode that lets you and your friends explore the ruins of a haunted ship together. We would never spoil this game’s best surprises, but it’s safe to say that Man of Medan offers an unforgettable horror multiplayer experience that may just change the way you look at your “friends” and whether or not you can ever really trust them again.

Resident Evil 3

You’re stuck in the middle of a zombie-filled city where the situation has gone from “bad” to “apocalyptic” in mere hours. As you try to escape, you realize you’re being hunted by a Terminator-like creature that won’t stop until you’re dead. Believe it or not, things only get worse from there. This remake of the underrated Resident Evil 3 fully-realizes the potential of one of the most intimidating set-ups in horror game history. While escaping from the legendary Nemesis creature that relentlessly pursues you makes up the bulk of the game’s best scares, Resident Evil 3’s surprisingly good action sequences ensure that you never feel entirely helpless.

The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners

Considered by many gamers to be one of the best VR games ever made, The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners also happens to be one of the few games based on The Walking Dead series that captures most of the things that made the franchise so popular in the first place. Yes, that means that Saints and Sinners lets you test your nerve against wave after wave of zombies, but the biggest surprises here are the moments when Saints & Sinners tasks you with deciding who you’re going to trust, what you’re going to do, and how you’ll survive.

Blair Witch

You’re definitely forgiven if you haven’t thought about the Blair Witch movie in years. After all, it’s been about 21 years since the release of the first film in the franchise, and the various sequels and reboots we’ve been treated to since then haven’t exactly lived up to its legacy. However, that’s exactly what you get in this truly terrifying revival of nearly everything that made the original film famous. As you dive deeper into this game’s dark woods, you start to realize that you’re only bringing yourself closer to the things that scare you most. Thankfully, the whole adventure is compelling enough to guarantee you won’t want to stop until you see the end.

Dead by Daylight

No matter what anyone tells you, it’s natural to root for the slasher. Yes, movies like Nightmare on Elm Street and Texas Chainsaw Massacre may expect you to cheer on the “heroes,” but we all know that most of us are in for the ride to see who is going to get killed next and how. Well, Dead by Daylight happens to be one of the only games that lets you step into the role of the slasher. Whether you’re playing as one of the four survivors or the killer from a roster of villains that includes horror legends like Freddy Krueger and Michael Myers, Dead by Daylight lets you see whether or not all of those things you found yourself shouting at the screen will actually pay off when you put them into practice.

Slender: The Arrival

In Slender, there's no fighting back, there's no outsmarting the enemy. There's just you, eight pages to collect, and a well-dressed being named Slender Man who's hell-bent on stopping you. When Slender Man first appeared in 2012 with Slender: The Eight Pages, gamers loved the simple premise and unpredictable scares. Slender: The Arrival expands on the original, with an additional enemy and levels that become increasingly difficult with each acquired page. Armed with nothing but a flashlight, players will want to leave the lights on for this game.

Alien: Isolation

Being stuck in space is already scary, but add in an alien killing machine and you've got the makings of a horrifying video game. Alien: Isolation follows Amanda Ripley, daughter of Ellen Ripley (of the Alien films). As she searches for answers to her mother's disappearance, Amanda must avoid and outsmart the alien creature using a motion detector and other tools on the spaceship. For fans of the original film series, Alien: Isolation is the perfect game for a nostalgic scare.

Five Nights at Freddy's: Help Wanted

[Most Terrifying](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/4672746145.html)

The eighth installment in the Five Nights franchise, Help Wanted brings the survival horror franchise to VR. As in the previous editions, players are a nighttime security guard who have to watch a kids' pizza place, while avoiding the living animatronics determined to kill them. With a headset on, there's truly nowhere to run in this installment. Plus, players have to do more than press buttons on a camera feed like in the original game. Help Wanted features new locations, new characters and a whole lot more scares.

The Evil Within 2

From Shinji Mikami, the same creator of the Resident Evil franchise, The Evil Within 2 follows Sebastian Castellanos, a former police officer. As in the original game, Castellanos must navigate an alternative reality called STEM. And now he's also searching for his daughter. The game features three different modes, and players can avoid enemies via stealth or direct combat. To navigate this nightmare realm, players will have to use all their gaming skills.

Observer

Krakow, Poland. 2084. A megacorporation has taken over and replaced regular police with Observers, who can hack people's minds and interrogate them. As detective Daniel Lazarski, players have to use their dystopian hacking skills to uncover a mystery. Observer's scares come from its visual effects and environment. Players have the ability to open a door inches at a time, adding to the suspense. Plus, hacking is more than just pressing a few buttons, and promises its own genuine scares. Observer is the perfect cyberpunk horror game.

Similar to the Amnesia series, Outlast's scares give you no way to fight back. It's all a deadly game of hide and seek, and you don't want to be found. In the game, a journalist breaks into an abandoned mental asylum to uncover its secrets. Little does he know, he's not alone. With just a camera to light his way, the character has to collect batteries and avoid enemies at all costs. There's no health bar, and no way to fight back. Prepare for jump scares and intense chase scenes.

Bloodborne

An action RPG may not be your first thought for a horror game, but Bloodborne uses gothic and survival horror to keep your on the edge of your seat. Death is hidden around every corner, and no amount of weapons will keep you completely safe. It's known for its difficulty, given Bloodborne is from the same people who created the almost-impossible Dark Souls games. If that's not enough to get you to play, there's rich storytelling, intense fights and an expansive map. What's not to like?

Layers of Fear

It doesn't take enemies to make a good horror game. Layers of Fear has players explore the Victorian mansion of a painter, and reveals his dark secrets. The game plays with players' heads, as rooms change and objects move before your eyes. It takes psychological horror seriously. Nothing is reliable, not even the narration. By the end, you may be questioning your own reality.

Phasmophobia

[Terrifying Horror Video](https://biopeptide.wiki/)

One of 2020’s best horror games is the utterly unexpected Phasmophobia. Essentially the multiplayer version of every ghost hunting show you ever started watching ironically, Phasmophobia tasks you and three friends with exploring a haunted location as part of a paranormal research experiment. What you’ll soon find is that having friends by your side does nothing to lessen that constant feeling of dread and the sense that something horrible lurks around every dark corner. Even skeptics will find themselves sweating as Phasmophobia waits to play its scariest tricks for when you least expect them.

World of Horror

Don’t be fooled by its retro graphics. Beneath that simple exterior, World of Horror hides a relentlessly terrifying experience based on classic adventure games, the stories of H.P. Lovecraft, and the art of Junji Ito. This apocalyptic story may overwhelm you with its tricky puzzles and downright weird story, but fans of psychological horror movies like Jacob’s Ladder and In the Mouth of Madness will fall in love with this game’s unrelenting scares and that constant feeling that you’re about to walk into something that you’re just not prepared for. Don’t miss out on this one if you’re looking for something different.

Amnesia: Rebirth

The original Amnesia set a new standard for truly scary horror games when it shocked the world in 2010, but it’s Amnesia: Rebirth that claims the crown as the scariest game in this franchise. After all, the original game may have thrust you into a nightmare that tested your sanity by constantly assaulting your senses, but Rebirth adds the very real dangers of a hostile desert environment that slowly drain you of your resources and will. This is not the kind of horror game you throw in when you’re looking to enjoy a few jump scares on your way to the credits. This is the kind of game you play when you want to see just how scary a game can be.

Pathologic 2

[Horror Video Games Ever](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/)

Pathologic 2 puts you in just about the most undesirable situation we can imagine. As the recently appointed healer in a town ravaged by a mysterious plague, you must somehow find a way to not only keep people alive but to ensure that you don’t die yourself. Complicating that already complicated scenario is the fact that the townspeople are restless and just as likely to turn against you as help you. While much of the horror of Pathologic 2 comes from making the best out of an unwinnable scenario, the lingering creepiness of the game’s design proves to be the set-up for some truly terrifying scares.

SOMA

In an underwater remote facility, protagonist Simon Jarrett tries to understand the history of the place and why he's there. Players have to solve puzzles, collect data and use their stealth skills to avoid monsters at all costs. If you want a horror game filled with story, this one's for you. SOMA provides ample backstory, which only adds to the fearful situation players are in. It's also from the same studio as Amnesia, so it's definitely going to have memorable scares.
