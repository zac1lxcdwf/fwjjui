How Stress Affects Immunity

When you experience stress—whether from work deadlines, family demands, or exams—your body goes into "fight or flight" mode. Key features include:

Hormone surge: Cortisol and adrenaline rise.

Inflammation: Short-term inflammation can become chronic.

Immune suppression: High cortisol levels decrease white blood cell activity.

Oxidative stress: An imbalance between free radicals and antioxidants damages cells.

Chronic stress leaves you more vulnerable to infections and slows recovery from illness.

The Power of Antioxidants

Antioxidants neutralize free radicals, reducing oxidative stress and supporting immune cells. Benefits include:

Protecting cell membranes and DNA

ACV and Improved Antioxidant Levels

Apple cider vinegar contains several bioactive compounds that contribute to its antioxidant potential:

Polyphenols: Natural plant compounds that scavenge free radicals.

Acetic acid: May modulate gut bacteria, indirectly supporting antioxidant uptake.

Flavonoids: Linked to reduced inflammation and improved immune response.

A small clinical study showed that individuals consuming ACV daily had measurable increases in total antioxidant capacity in their blood. While more research is needed, these findings suggest ACV can be part of an antioxidant-rich regimen under medical guidance.

What Your Doctor Wants You to Know

Evidence is emerging: ACV shows promise, but large-scale human trials are limited.

Not a cure: ACV is an adjunct, not a substitute for vaccines, medications, or medical care.

Dosing matters: Typical safe amounts are 1–2 tablespoons (15–30 mL) diluted in water, once or twice daily.

Timing and mixing: Taking ACV before meals may aid digestion and blood sugar balance.

Quality counts: Choose raw, unfiltered ACV with "the mother" for maximum polyphenol content.

Always discuss new supplements with your healthcare provider to avoid interactions with medications (e.g., diuretics, insulin) and to address individual health concerns.

Safe Ways to Use ACV

Dilute thoroughly: Undiluted vinegar can irritate the throat and tooth enamel.

Use a straw: Minimizes contact with teeth.

Start slow: Begin with 1 teaspoon (5 mL) diluted, then increase if well tolerated.

Monitor symptoms: Note any heartburn, digestive upset, or low potassium levels.

Avoid at bedtime: May disrupt sleep if acid reflux occurs.

Complementary Strategies to Reduce Stress

ACV works best as part of a broader stress-management plan. Consider:

Balanced diet: Focus on colorful fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains.

Regular exercise: Even 20 minutes of walking boosts mood and immunity.

Mindfulness and relaxation: Meditation, deep breathing, or yoga can lower cortisol.

Adequate sleep: Aim for 7–9 hours per night to restore immune cells.

Social support: Connecting with friends and family buffers stress.
